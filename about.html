Studying Mars.

Mission objectives

What can we learn on Mars?

Mars has a thin carbon-dioxide atmosphere, a cold and dusty surface, and evidence of water ice. These conditions pose significant challenges for crew health and equipment.

AURORA is a fictional concept for examining the planet and the systems and procedures required for crewed surface work.

Martian conditions

Dust, cold, and water ice.

Water ice is present at Mars's poles and beneath parts of its surface. Its distribution, accessibility, and potential uses remain important research questions; this concept does not assume a particular landing site or mission finding.

Mission priorities

Science, safety, and reporting.

The concept includes both the scientific work and the conditions required to carry it out.

01 / SCIENCE

Collect reliable data

Use repeatable measurements and distinguish observed results from untested assumptions.

02 / SAFETY

Plan for crew health

Consider medical needs, equipment reliability, resource limits, and the Martian environment.

03 / REPORTING

Document outcomes

Record methods, findings, and limitations so others can understand and assess the work.

Mission development

Key stages before a crewed mission.

Step 01

Survey

Use orbital and robotic observations to characterize terrain and identify hazards.

Step 02

Test

Evaluate the landing plan, equipment, habitat, and emergency procedures under relevant conditions.

Step 03

Assess readiness

A crewed mission would depend on the results of earlier testing and safety reviews.
